Frequently asked questions

Why trade gold (XAU/USD) instead of forex pairs?
Gold offers traders exposure to a globally-recognized safe haven asset that moves based on different drivers than currency pairs. Gold responds to inflation data, central bank policy, geopolitical tensions, and dollar strength. Gold also has more volatility than most major forex pairs, creating more trading opportunities but also higher risk.
What spread should I expect on XAU/USD?
Gold spreads vary by broker but typically range from $0.20 to $0.40 per ounce (20-40 cents) at reputable brokers during normal market conditions. Spreads widen significantly during news events, market opens, and low-liquidity periods. Always check your broker's spread before trading gold-based signals.
How much capital do I need for gold trading?
Gold trading typically requires more capital than forex pairs due to larger pip values and higher volatility. A 0.1 lot gold position can result in approximately $1 per pip movement. Most professional gold traders recommend at least $5,000 to $10,000 minimum for prop capital, though smaller starts are possible with appropriate position sizing.
Are paid gold signals more reliable than free signals?
Not necessarily. Several free providers in this ranking (Golden Banker, Gold Mania, Gold Flower, UnitedSignals) have demonstrated win rates competitive with or exceeding premium services. Pricing reflects business model choices — community building, educational depth, lifestyle branding — more than signal reliability. Independent verification matters more than paid versus free status.
What's the difference between gold scalping and swing signals?
Gold scalping signals target 10-30 pip moves on very short timeframes (minutes to hours), while swing signals aim for 50-300+ pip moves held for days or weeks. Scalping requires more screen time and tighter stops; swing trading requires patience and wider stops. Choose based on your available time and risk tolerance.
How do I verify a gold signal provider's claimed performance?
The most reliable verification methods include: MyFxBook verified accounts (where gold trades are specifically tracked), TradingView verified profiles with historical signal records, and independent third-party tracking from publications like TelegramSignalsReviews. Self-reported performance figures should be treated with skepticism, especially when they exceed 80% claimed accuracy without third-party validation.
